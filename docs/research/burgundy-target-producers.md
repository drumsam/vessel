# The ten producers your clients want — identity register and pricing route

23 October 2026. Written for buy-side evaluation: a supplier or private seller
offers wine, and you need to know in minutes whether it works.

**Read the identity section before the pricing section.** On this list a name
confusion is a 5–20× error, and it goes the wrong way: you pay Domaine Leflaive
money for Olivier Leflaive wine, or you decline a real Coche-Dury because the
offer said "Coche".

---

## Part 1 · The identity traps

These are the ten your clients named, what they almost certainly mean, and what
will be offered to you by mistake or by design.

### 1. DRC — Domaine de la Romanée-Conti

**The trap is the cuvée, not the producer.** Two of its wines are monopoles and
can only be DRC: **Romanée-Conti** and **La Tâche**. Everything else it makes is
also made by other people:

- **Romanée-St-Vivant** — DRC, but also Leroy, Cathiard, Arnoux-Lachaux, Dujac,
  Hudelot-Noëllat, Confuron
- **Richebourg** — DRC, but also Leroy, Méo-Camuzet, Gros, Grivot, Hudelot-Noëllat
- **Grands-Échézeaux / Échézeaux** — DRC, but also many
- **Corton** — DRC since 2009, and dozens of others
- **Montrachet** — DRC, and a dozen others

So an offer reading "Richebourg 2015, 3 bottles" tells you almost nothing.
**Never price a DRC cuvée name without the domaine on the label.**

One further confusion worth knowing: **La Romanée** is a different monopole
belonging to **Comte Liger-Belair**, not DRC. The names are one letter apart in
conversation and an order of magnitude apart in some vintages.

DRC's village wine is **Vosne-Romanée Cuvée Duvault-Blochet**, made only in
abundant years — genuinely DRC, far cheaper than the grands crus, and sometimes
offered as though it carried grand cru weight.

### 2. Leroy — three separate businesses, one surname

**This is the single most expensive confusion in Burgundy.** Lalou Bize-Leroy is
behind two of the three, and they are not the same wine or the same price.

| Entity | What it is | Relative price |
|---|---|---|
| **Domaine Leroy** | Lalou's own vineyards, bought from Charles Noëllat in 1988, ~22ha, biodynamic | the astronomical one |
| **Domaine d'Auvenay** | Lalou's *personal* estate, separate holding, tiny volumes | astronomical, often more |
| **Maison Leroy** | the négociant house, founded 1868, buys in fruit and wine | a fraction |

A label reading just **"Leroy"** is ambiguous and must be resolved before any
number is attached to it. **Maison Leroy** bottles are legitimate and
well-regarded — they are simply a different market. If an offer says "Leroy
Chambertin" at a price that looks too good, it is probably Maison, or it is
probably not real.

### 3. D'Auvenay — Domaine d'Auvenay

Lalou's separate estate, and your clients named it separately from Leroy, which
tells me they know the distinction. Mostly whites — **Meursault, Chevalier-
Montrachet, Criots-Bâtard, Puligny Les Folatières** — plus a few reds including
**Mazis-Chambertin** and **Bonnes-Mares**. Volumes are minute, a few hundred
bottles of some cuvées. **Provenance matters more here than almost anywhere**,
because the scarcity and the price make it a target.

### 4. Bizot — Domaine Jean-Yves Bizot

Vosne-Romanée. Tiny domaine, cult following, **Échézeaux** and **Vosne-Romanée
Les Réas** the best known.

**The trap: Coche-Bizouard.** Different family, different village, a fraction of
the price. In a hand-typed list "Bizot" and "Bizouard" are a glance apart.

### 5. Roumier — Domaine Georges Roumier

Chambolle-Musigny. **Musigny, Bonnes-Mares, Amoureuses, Cras.**

- **Christophe Roumier** is the winemaker *at* Georges Roumier — the same estate.
  An offer naming him is the right wine.
- **Hervé Roumier** is a **different** Chambolle domaine, much cheaper.
- **Laurent Roumier** likewise different.

So "Roumier Bonnes-Mares" needs the first name or the word Georges before it
carries a Georges Roumier price.

### 6. Lafon — Domaine des Comtes Lafon

Meursault. **Montrachet, Meursault Perrières / Charmes / Genevrières, Volnay
Santenots-du-Milieu.**

Two traps, both in the same family:

- **Héritiers du Comte Lafon** — the family's **Mâcon** estate. Perfectly good
  wine, a small fraction of the price. The name is nearly identical.
- **Dominique Lafon** — his own separate négoce label, not the domaine.

### 7. Coche — Coche-Dury

Meursault. **Corton-Charlemagne, Meursault Perrières, Meursault Caillerets**,
and even the straight Bourgogne Blanc trades at a serious number.

- **Coche-Bizouard** — different producer, cheap by comparison.
- **Fabien Coche** — the renamed Coche-Bizouard estate. Still not Coche-Dury.

**An offer that says only "Coche" is unpriceable.** Ask which.

### 8. PYCM — Pierre-Yves Colin-Morey

Chassagne / Saint-Aubin / Puligny. The Colin family is large and the names are
close. All of these are different producers at different prices:

**Marc Colin** (the father's estate) · **Pierre-Yves Colin-Morey** · **Philippe
Colin** · **Bruno Colin** · **Joseph Colin** · **Simon Colin** · **Colin-Deléger**

PYCM also runs a **négoce** arm alongside the domaine bottlings, which price
differently. The label distinction is worth learning if you trade these often.

### 9. Leflaive — Domaine Leflaive

Puligny-Montrachet. **Chevalier-Montrachet, Bâtard, Bienvenues-Bâtard,
Pucelles, Folatières.**

**The trap: Olivier Leflaive.** A separate, respected négociant house in the
same village, run by a cousin, selling at a small fraction of the domaine's
prices. "Leflaive Puligny 1er Cru" with no qualifier is ambiguous, and the two
readings are many multiples apart.

### 10. Rousseau — Domaine Armand Rousseau

Gevrey-Chambertin. The producer is rarely confused; **the tiers within it are**,
and the gaps are large:

**Chambertin** and **Clos de Bèze** sit well above **Clos St-Jacques**, which
sits well above **Cazetiers / Lavaux St-Jacques / Charmes**, which sit well
above **Gevrey-Chambertin village**.

A list saying "Rousseau Gevrey 2015" could be any of five wines across a very
wide range. Get the cuvée before you quote.

## Part 2 · The pricing route for this list

Here is the honest problem. **These are precisely the wines UK merchant pages
cannot price**, and for a structural reason: they are allocated, they sell on
release, and a merchant's public catalogue shows what it currently has rather
than what the market is doing. Our Champagne sweep across seven UK merchants
reached two independent sources on 11 of 52 targets — and Champagne is far more
widely stocked than DRC.

So the route is different for this list:

**Auction is the market for these producers**, not merchant asks. Executed
hammer prices, with dates, are both more available and better evidence —
somebody actually paid that.

This makes **Wine Market Journal Professional** the answer rather than an
option: USD 349.95 a year, auction results from 1997 to present, unlimited
searches, and it covers the houses where these wines actually trade. HK$2,730
against a single DRC line is nothing.

**There is a free 24-hour trial with ten searches.** Spend it on this list, not
on Champagne:

1. Domaine Leroy Chambertin
2. Domaine d'Auvenay Meursault Les Narvaux
3. Coche-Dury Corton-Charlemagne
4. Georges Roumier Bonnes-Mares
5. DRC Richebourg
6. Armand Rousseau Clos St-Jacques
7. Domaine Leflaive Chevalier-Montrachet
8. Comtes Lafon Meursault Perrières
9. Jean-Yves Bizot Échézeaux
10. PYCM Chassagne Caillerets

If those ten come back with recent, dated hammer prices, you have a pricing
source for your clients' entire want list and the merchant-adapter problem
stops mattering for this part of the business.

Second source worth having for Asia: **Hong Kong and Asian auction results**,
where this list trades heavily and sometimes above Europe.

## Part 3 · The workflow this needs, which is not what we built

Everything built so far runs **sell-side**: a cellar arrives, we price all of
it, we offer it to buyers. That is a batch process measured in hours.

What you are describing is **buy-side and single-line**: a supplier messages you
with three bottles, and you need an answer in minutes before someone else takes
them.

Different questions:

| Sell-side (built) | Buy-side (needed) |
|---|---|
| What is this cellar worth? | Is this one ask below market? |
| Price everything, then curate | Price one line, now |
| 48 minutes for 52 targets | 30 seconds for 1 line |
| Output: an offer document | Output: buy / pass, and at what |
| Evidence for the client | Evidence for me |

The screen this wants is a **single box**: paste or type what you were offered,
the system resolves the identity, asks you to confirm which producer when the
name is ambiguous, shows the auction and merchant evidence it can find, and
answers two things — **what it's worth** and **what you could resell it at**.

That's a smaller build than the Cellar Desk and it is probably more use to you
more often, because an inbound offer arrives far more frequently than a 561-line
cellar.

## A caveat on this document

Part 1 is from my own knowledge of these estates, checked where I could. The
structural facts — Domaine versus Maison Leroy, Domaine versus Olivier Leflaive,
the Colin family, Coche-Dury versus Coche-Bizouard — I am confident about.

Where you should trust yourself over me: **current ownership and any recent
renaming or splitting of estates.** Those change, and you trade these wines.
Correct anything here that your own experience contradicts, and it becomes the
register the identity layer is built from.
